## 2. English press release (~600 words) — tech angle

> **Angle**: "Hebrew-first AI book platform launches for memorial-book niche"
> **Distribution**: TechCrunch (Israel desk), The Next Web, VentureBeat, Times of Israel, Haaretz English, ZDNET, Geektime English edition
> **Lead**: data-driven; the technical and market gap.

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
[Date]

Israeli Startup Launches Hebrew-First AI Platform for Memorial Books and Family Histories

TEL AVIV — Hebrew-language users have long been an afterthought in the AI writing tools market. Major platforms — Wattpad, Reedsy, Sudowrite — are English-first, with Hebrew support ranging from poor to nonexistent. MeStory, a self-funded Israeli startup founded in 2024, is the first AI book platform built Hebrew-first from the ground up, with full English support as a second language.

The opportunity, according to MeStory, is sharper than the headline numbers suggest. Israel has roughly 8 million Hebrew speakers and a high per-capita rate of family-history projects, biographies, and memorial books — but no comparable purpose-built tooling. Memorial books (ספרי הנצחה in Hebrew) are a particularly underserved niche: traditional self-publishing platforms aren't optimized for the use case, and AI tools that generate fiction don't transfer well to a project built from voice interviews with elderly relatives.

"What's missing isn't 'an AI writing tool,'" said Maor Argaman, MeStory's founder. "There are dozens of those. What's missing is software that takes a four-hour interview with someone's grandmother in Hebrew, transcribes it accurately, helps the family structure those memories into chapters, designs a book cover that does justice to a life, and produces a finished, printable book — without forcing the user to assemble seven different tools."

MeStory's platform combines four traditionally separate workflows in a single end-to-end product:

- **AI-guided writing** with chapter structure suggestions, draft polishing, and Hebrew-English translation that preserves the author's voice
- **Voice transcription** in Hebrew and English, optimized for noisy or older recordings and for elderly speakers with strong accents
- **Cover and layout design** with templates for memorial books, autobiographies, novels, and children's books, all RTL-native
- **A built-in marketplace** where finished books can be published and sold, with authors keeping the majority of each sale

The platform is freemium, with paid tiers at $9.99 and $19.99/month. Authors retain full ownership of their content; MeStory does not use user manuscripts to train models.

Technical foundation: React + Vite frontend, Vercel deployment, OpenAI for transcription, Google Gemini for text generation. Available as a Progressive Web App and as native iOS / Android apps via Capacitor.

The startup also offers a free, time-unlimited Pro tier to bereaved Israeli families writing memorial books for fallen soldiers — accessible by personal email request rather than a generic signup form.

Argaman noted that the company is bootstrapped, focused on product over fundraising, and not currently raising capital. "We've made the deliberate choice to grow on revenue and on the strength of the product, rather than on a runway dictated by an investor's clock," he said. "For the kind of work memorial-book authors do, that pace makes more sense."

A second technical detail Argaman highlighted: MeStory does not use customer manuscripts to train AI models. "Many writing-tool companies are quietly using user content to fine-tune their models," he said. "We don't. The book the family writes is theirs, end-to-end. That seemed obvious to us — but in this market, it's not the default."

"This is a market with no obvious global competitor that's gotten Hebrew right," he said. "Wattpad is fundamentally a community for English-language fiction. Reedsy is a marketplace for English-speaking freelance editors. Storybird is for children's stories. None of them serve a 70-year-old Israeli engineer who wants to write his autobiography in Hebrew, or a granddaughter trying to record her grandmother's life in time."

About MeStory:
Founded in 2024, based in Tel Aviv. Self-funded. Hebrew-first AI book writing, design, and publishing platform with full English support. Web at mestory-ai.com.
```

## 5. Press kit checklist

Before sending any pitch, have **all** of the following ready as a single shareable folder (Google Drive, Dropbox, or a `/press` page on your site).

### Essential

- ☐ **Logo files**: PNG (transparent background) at 512×512 and 1200×1200; SVG; both light and dark variants if available
- ☐ **Open Graph image**: 1200×630, branded
- ☐ **Founder photo**: high-res (1200×1200+), professional headshot, both color and black-and-white
- ☐ **Founder bio**: short (50 words), medium (150 words), long (300 words)
- ☐ **Company fact sheet**: one-page PDF with founding date, location, employee count, funding, key milestones, contact
- ☐ **Product screenshots**: at least 8 high-res (1920×1200+), no Lorem Ipsum, with both Hebrew and English visible
- ☐ **30-second product video**: with captions in English (and Hebrew if possible)
- ☐ **Both press releases** (Hebrew and English, in PDF + plain text)
- ☐ **Pricing summary**: clean one-pager

### Strongly recommended

- ☐ **Two anonymized customer stories**: 200 words each, with photos if family permits, in Hebrew and English
- ☐ **A "before / after" demo**: a one-minute clip showing a raw voice interview being transcribed and shaped into a book chapter
- ☐ **Social media handles**: links to Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn (Twitter/X if active)
- ☐ **Schema.org JSON-LD snippet** for the press page itself

### Nice to have

- ☐ **A founder podcast clip** (5–10 min) discussing the product and motivation
- ☐ **Quotes from real users** (with written permission) — 2–3 short testimonial paragraphs
- ☐ **An existing "early access" or "private beta" customer list** to demonstrate traction (numbers OK; names not without consent)

## 6. Cadence and follow-up

### Week 1
- Day 1: Pitch top 5 (highest-confidence reporters first)
- Day 3: Pitch next 5
- Day 5: Pitch remaining ~12
- Friday: Soft check-in with non-responders from Day 1 (one short follow-up only — *do not* re-pitch the same email)

### Week 2
- Continue follow-ups
- If a reporter expresses interest, prepare for a 15–20 min call within 48 hours
- Track each pitch in a simple sheet: outlet | reporter | date pitched | response | status

### Don't
- Send mass emails. Reporters can tell.
- Use first names you haven't verified.
- Follow up more than once.
- Pitch the same reporter again after rejection within 6 months.
